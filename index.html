Coaching Philosophy

Mindful Movements Matter

Atlas coaching is centered on helping athletes understand what they are trying to do in each lift, why a position matters, and how to make technical work usable under load.

The goal is durable progress: better positions, clearer feedback, smarter training decisions, and movement that supports the athlete beyond one heavy attempt.

MEET COACH SHEN

How the approach shows up in training.

Technique and positions

Teach the position. Then teach the athlete to use it.

Coach Shen teaches the Olympic lifts by breaking down the positions, timing, and movement patterns that make up the full lift. Rather than relying on one cue for every athlete, she looks at where the lift is breaking down and chooses mobility work, technical drills, or strength work around that specific problem.

The goal is to give the athlete a position they understand and can reproduce as the lift gets faster and heavier.

Ankle range Overhead stability Front rack

Mobility

Create access to the position.

When mobility limits a position used in the snatch or clean and jerk, Coach Shen addresses that limitation as part of the training session. The goal is not flexibility for its own sake. It is giving the athlete access to a position they need to lift effectively.

Technique

Use drills to solve what she sees.

Technical variations are selected around the problem in front of her. That may mean working on bar path, pull timing, footwork, turnover, dip-drive rhythm, or the receiving position rather than adding drills without a specific reason.

01 Position

Find the position the athlete needs. Mobility and setup work are used when the athlete cannot consistently access a position required by the lift.

02 Practice

Give the athlete opportunities to repeat it. Technical drills isolate parts of the movement so Coach Shen can reinforce the position, timing, or pattern being worked on.

03 Apply

Bring it back into the full lift. The change matters when the athlete can carry it into the snatch or clean and jerk as speed and load increase.

Training approach

Why Atlas uses a Chinese-influenced approach.

Weightlifting systems can place very different emphasis on intensity, exercise selection, positions, and technical development. Atlas uses a Chinese-influenced approach. The comparisons below show how that approach differs from two other commonly discussed systems.

Bulgarian approach

Heavy, specific, and high intensity.

Bulgarian-style training is commonly associated with frequent exposure to heavy competition lifts, limited exercise variation, and high training intensity.

  • Frequent heavy classical lifts.
  • Relatively narrow exercise selection in its best-known forms.
  • High recovery demands from repeated high-intensity exposure.

Compared with the approach used at Atlas, it places less emphasis on extensive accessory work and technical variation.

American approaches

More varied and periodized.

American weightlifting programs vary considerably, but many use planned training blocks, broader exercise selection, and changing levels of volume and intensity.

  • Broader exercise selection.
  • Planned changes in training volume and intensity.
  • Programming can differ substantially between coaches and systems.

Compared with Atlas, American weightlifting is less defined by one technical methodology and can vary significantly from coach to coach.

ATLAS METHOD

Chinese-influenced approach

Technique, positions, mobility, and supporting strength.

Atlas follows a Chinese-influenced approach that gives substantial attention to technical positions, mobility, accessory strength work, and repetition of the movements that support the snatch and clean and jerk.

  • Detailed technical work.
  • Mobility when positions require it.
  • Accessory work selected around weaknesses or technical needs.
  • Positional and leg strength developed alongside the competition lifts.

How Coach Shen applies it

Chinese-influenced technique, adapted to the athlete.

Coach Shen uses Chinese-influenced principles as the foundation of her coaching. She emphasizes technical detail, positional strength, mobility, and accessory work, then adjusts those tools around the athlete's proportions, experience, current limitations, and training goals.

  • Repeatable technical positions.
  • Mobility that supports the lifts.
  • Accessory work chosen for a purpose.
  • Strength developed around the demands of weightlifting.
